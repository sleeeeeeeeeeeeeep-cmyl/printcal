Step 1 of 5:PID tuning

PID_CALIBRATEM303

Keeps the nozzle or bed at a steady temperature. Only needed after you change the hotend, heater, thermistor, silicone sock or part-cooling fan.

  1. Let the heater cool to room temperature.
  2. Pick the heater and the temperature you print at most.
  3. Copy the command, send it to the printer, and stay with it while it runs. PrintCal checks the target is safe; the printer does the measuring.
PID_CALIBRATE HEATER=… TARGET=…, then SAVE_CONFIGM303 E<heater> S<temp> C<cycles> U1, then M500Klipper docs ↗ · Marlin M303 ↗

Step 2 of 5:E-steps calculator

rotation_distancee-stepsM92

Makes the extruder push exactly the length of filament it's asked to. Fixes over- or under-extrusion on every print.

  1. Heat the nozzle to printing temperature.
  2. Mark the filament above the extruder and measure from the extruder to the mark.
  3. Extrude slowly with G91 then G1 E50 F60 (not the screen's extrude button), measure to the mark again, and type both distances below.

The current value is filled in from your preset. Replace it with the value your printer actually uses (from your printer.cfgthe M92 … E number from M503).

new rotation_distance = current × actual ÷ requestednew e-steps = current × requested ÷ actual · actual = initial − afterKlipper docs ↗ · Ellis' guide ↗

Step 3 of 5:Pressure advance / Linear Advance

TUNING_TOWERcorners and seamsOrcaSlicer PA towerM900

Sharpens corners and closes gaps at seams.

  1. Print a pressure advance tower (the commands are below for Klipper, or use OrcaSlicer's PA tower).
  2. Find the height where the corners look sharpest. If you can't decide, pick the lower one.
  3. Measure that height with calipers and type it below.

Klipper tuning tower

OrcaSlicer PA tower (works for Klipper and Marlin)

The value rises one step per millimetre of height.

PA = start + height × factorKlipper docs ↗ · OrcaSlicer wiki ↗

Step 4 of 5:Flow ratio

OrcaSlicerBambu StudioCura / Prusa flow %

Fine-tunes how much plastic your slicer asks for, for each filament. Do the extruder first.

  1. Print OrcaSlicer's flow rate test (Pass 1, then Pass 2).
  2. Pick the block with the smoothest top surface.
  3. Type its number below and put the result in your filament profile.

Cura and PrusaSlicer show flow as a percentage: 0.97 is 97 %.

OrcaSlicer flow rate test

Pass 1 goes from −20 to +20 in steps of 5. Pass 2 goes from −9 to 0 in steps of 1.

Using a different flow test? (OrcaSlicer YOLO, single-wall cube)

OrcaSlicer YOLO (2.3+)

The modifier is added, not multiplied. Recommended range ±0.05 in 0.01 steps.

Single-wall cube (any slicer)

2-pass: old × (100 + mod) ÷ 100 · YOLO: old + mod · wall: old × target ÷ meanOrcaSlicer wiki ↗

Step 5 of 5:Max volumetric speed

OrcaSlicermm³/s

Finds how fast your hotend can melt plastic, so the slicer never asks for more.

  1. Print OrcaSlicer's max flowrate test.
  2. Find the height where the walls first get gaps or turn rough.
  3. Type it below. PrintCal turns it into a safe limit and a top printing speed.
MVS = start + height × step · area = h × (w − h(1 − π/4)) · speed = MVS ÷ areaOrcaSlicer wiki ↗ · Flow.cpp ↗

Temperature & retraction towers

temp towerretraction tower

Finds the best temperature or retraction length for a filament.

  1. Pick Temperature or Retraction and set the tower's range. PrintCal checks it's safe before you print.
  2. Print it and pick the best-looking block.
  3. Measure that block's height to see the setting it was printed with.

For OrcaSlicer's retraction tower, confirm the values by searching the G-code preview for Calib_Retraction_tower.

value = bottom ± step × ⌊(h − base) ÷ every⌋, clamped to the tower rangeOrcaSlicer retraction ↗

Input shaper frequency

ringingghostingringing towerSHAPER_CALIBRATEM593

Removes the ripples (ghosting) that echo after sharp corners.

  1. Print Klipper's ringing_tower.stl. It works for any firmware.
  2. On each axis, measure a distance D across N ripples, skipping the first one or two after the notch.
  3. Type the outer-wall speed, ripple count and distance below.

Marlin M593 frequency tower

For the tower in Marlin's M593 docs: the slicer raises the frequency every layer with M593 F{start + span × (layer − 2) ÷ layers}. Measure the cleanest height.

f = V × N ÷ DKlipper docs ↗ · Marlin M593 ↗

Skew correction

SET_SKEWskew_correctionKlipper

Fixes parts that print slightly diamond-shaped instead of square. Klipper only.

  1. Print a skew calibration square.
  2. Measure diagonal AC, diagonal BD and side AD, without the corner flats.
  3. Type them below.

Your printer.cfg needs a [skew_correction] section first.

side = √(2AC² + 2BD² − 4AD²) ÷ 2 · factor = tan(π/2 − acos((AC² − side² − AD²) ÷ (2·side·AD)))Klipper docs ↗

Shrinkage & scale

OrcaSlicerABS / ASA / PETG

Corrects parts that come out a little too small, common with ABS, ASA and PETG.

  1. Print a large calibration cube. A 100 mm cube is more accurate than a 20 mm one.
  2. Measure X, Y and Z.
  3. Type them below and use the result in your slicer.

Fix the extruder and flow ratio first, or this will hide their errors.

Orca shrink % = measured ÷ nominal × 100 · scale % = nominal ÷ measured × 100OrcaSlicer source ↗

Axis steps (X / Y / Z)

rotation_distancesteps/mmreference only

Checks the X, Y and Z values after a new mainboard, a fresh Klipper install or a rebuilt axis.

Enter your belt or leadscrew details to get the number your config should have. These come from the hardware, so never adjust them from test prints. PrintCal shows the value to compare; it doesn't generate axis config lines.

rotation_distance = belt pitch × pulley teeth · = screw pitch × thread starts · steps/mm = motor steps × microsteps ÷ rotation_distanceKlipper docs ↗

Filament left on the spool

PLAPETGABSTPU

Tells you whether the filament left on a spool will finish a print.

  1. Weigh the spool.
  2. Enter the empty spool's weight (weigh one, or check the brand's spec).
  3. Enter the length your slicer says the print needs.

Densities are typical values. Use the datasheet if you have it.

length = mass ÷ (ρ × π × (d/2)²)

How PrintCal works

You print a test, measure it, and type the measurement in. PrintCal works out the setting, checks it against your hardware, and gives you the exact command or config line. It never talks to your printer: nothing changes until you paste something in yourself.

  1. Describe your printer. Pick the model, firmware and extruder in the sentence at the top of the page, then pick a problem under What do you want to fix? Starting values, the commands shown (Klipper or Marlin) and every safety limit follow from this.
  2. Calibrate in order: PID tuning (only after a hotend, heater or fan change) → extruder → pressure advance → flow ratio → max volumetric speed, then towers, input shaper, skew and shrinkage as you need them. Each step assumes the earlier ones are right. A flow fix can't make up for a wrong extruder value; it just hides it.
  3. Replace the example numbers. Every calculator starts with sample measurements so you can see how it works. Type yours over them. Until you do, that result stays out of "All changes".
  4. Test, then save. Klipper results come in pairs: a live command to try now (forgotten on restart) and the line to make permanent in printer.cfg. Marlin results end with M500, which saves them.
  5. Apply everything at once. All changes collects every result that passed its checks.
What PrintCal will and won't change
It gives you
  • Single keys to change inside sections you already have (rotation_distance, pressure_advance, shaper_freq_x/y)
  • Live test commands that are lost on restart, so you can try a value before saving it
  • Marlin M92 / M900 / M593 followed by M500
It never touches
  • Pins, endstops, position_min/max, homing, probe offsets, thermistor types or driver currents
  • Whole config files to paste over yours
  • Any value that fails its range check, unless you press override
Reading the results
  • Blue-tinted input: a value PrintCal filled in from your preset. It's a starting point; overwrite it with what your printer actually uses.
  • Readout: the main result of each calculator is shown like a digital caliper display. The smaller numbers next to it are supporting values.
  • OK: inside the expected range for your setup.
  • ! (warning): the result is shown, but something is unusual. Read it before applying.
  • ✕ (blocked): the result is hidden because it's outside a safe range. That almost always means a measuring or mechanical problem, so fix that and re-measure. The override button shows the value anyway if you're sure.
  • The line at the bottom of each calculator: the exact maths used, with a link to the documentation it comes from.
  • Copy copies exactly the text shown in that box. Lines starting with # (config) or ; (console) are comments and are safe to paste.
Customizing it for your setup
  • Extruder not in the list? Choose Other / custom extruder, then type the value your printer uses now into the extruder calculator (Klipper: rotation_distance from printer.cfg; Marlin: the E number from M503). The "doesn't match the preset" check is skipped; every other check still runs.
  • Upgraded hotend? Set Hotend to all-metal and enter the max_temp from your firmware. The temperature-tower cap becomes max_temp − 15 °C instead of 240 °C.
  • Different nozzle? Pick its size in setup. The example line width, layer height and wall target follow it until you type your own.
  • Different motor or microsteps? Set them in setup. Marlin e-steps scale with both. Klipper's rotation_distance doesn't; Klipper reads them from microsteps and full_steps_per_rotation instead.
  • The gear_ratio box (Klipper only): tick it only if your [extruder] section has a gear_ratio: line. PrintCal ticks it when the extruder maker or the official config uses one, so check it against your file.
  • Self-built Marlin with LIN_ADVANCE or INPUT_SHAPING: pick Custom Marlin 2.1.2+.
  • Accelerometer (Klipper only): pick it in setup and the input-shaper card shows the automatic SHAPER_CALIBRATE route.

What's saved, and where
  • Your setup and measurements are saved in this browser only (local storage), so they're still there next visit. Nothing is uploaded, and there's no tracking.
  • A different browser or device starts fresh. Reset everything at the bottom of the page clears it all.
Running your own copy: adding printers, extruders and limits
  • PrintCal is one index.html file. Save it, edit it in any text editor, and open it in a browser. To publish it, put it in a GitHub repository and turn on GitHub Pages.
  • Everything you'd want to change is in one block near the end of the file, marked CUSTOMIZE HERE: the PRINTERS list, the EXTRUDERS list, the safety LIMITS and the DEFAULT_PRINTER. Each preset is one line. Copy a similar line, change it, save, reload.
  • Every field is explained in the comment above the lists. Only id and name are required; anything you leave out falls back to a safe default.
  • If an entry has a mistake (a typo in drive, a gear written 42/12 instead of 42:12, a duplicate id), PrintCal skips that one entry and lists the problem in a red box at the top of the page. It never calculates with a broken preset.

Questions

What is the Ender 3 V2 / Ender 3 Pro rotation_distance in Klipper?

Klipper's official configs for both use rotation_distance: 34.406 for the stock extruder, about 93 steps/mm. Treat it as a starting point and calibrate with the extruder section above.

What's the Ender 3 S1 rotation_distance and gear_ratio?

The official S1 config uses rotation_distance: 26.359 with gear_ratio: 42:12 (about 424.9 steps/mm). Without the gear_ratio line the equivalent value is about 7.53. Keep the pair together.

I put a Sprite Extruder SE on my Ender 3 Neo. What do I change?

Pick Ender 3 Neo and Creality Sprite Extruder SE in the setup. Creality's manual gives 424.9 e-steps. In Klipper that's gear_ratio: 42:12 + rotation_distance: 26.359 (or 7.531 with no gear_ratio line). Add the gear_ratio line if your old bowden config didn't have one, or extrusion will be about 3.5× too low. Then check direction, cut retraction below 3 mm, redo pressure advance and re-measure probe offsets.

I installed a Micro Swiss NG / Orbiter / BMG. What should I start with?

Choose it under Extruder in the setup. PrintCal fills in the manufacturer's starting value (NG 400, Orbiter v2.0 690, BMG 415 steps/mm) and converts it for Klipper. Then run the extruder test.

What are the Ender 3 X, Y and Z rotation_distance values?

Every official Klipper Ender config uses rotation_distance: 40 for X and Y (2 mm GT2 belt × 20-tooth pulley, 80 steps/mm) and 8 for Z (400 steps/mm). The Ender 5 Pro and Ender 5 Plus use 4 for Z (800 steps/mm). They come from the hardware, so don't calibrate them from test prints.

Why did PrintCal block my result?

Results that move the setting by more than 25 %, exceed your hotend's temperature cap, or fall outside documented ranges (pressure advance above 1.0, for example) usually mean a measurement or mechanical problem. A slipping extruder, a partial clog or the wrong gear_ratio are typical. Fix the cause and re-measure. You can still override if you're sure.

Can I tune input shaping on a stock Ender without an accelerometer?

On Klipper, yes: use the ringing tower. On Marlin, input shaping needs firmware built with INPUT_SHAPING (Marlin 2.1.2+). Send M593: if it replies "Unknown command", your firmware doesn't have it.
